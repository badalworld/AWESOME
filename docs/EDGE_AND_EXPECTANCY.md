## 1. The target, in arithmetic

* $20 → $10,000 in 7 days = **143.0 %/day** compounded (500x total).
* ⚠️ Anything above ~10 %/day *sustained* is outside what a 5m divergence strategy can produce; +143 %/day means the plan itself is the problem, not the settings.
* One full take-profit at +200 % ROI on 8 % margin at 10x = **+16.0 % equity**.
* A stopped-out trade at ~45 % ROI = **-3.6 % equity** (3 × ATR(14) on a volatile 5m alt, ~10x, plus slippage/fees).
* So the target needs **≈ 42 clean TP hits in a row with zero losses**, or a far larger number of trailing-stop wins.

Read it as: the strategy can compound a small account, slowly, with a real chance of the account going nowhere or down over short windows. It cannot turn $20 into $10,000 in a week — no settings, no leverage and no bot can, because that requires the market to hand you a 500x in 7 days.

### What a trailing-stop win is actually worth

+200 % ROI at 10x means the *price* must move **20 %** in your favour without first triggering the trail or the stop. On the 5m chart most positions are finished long before that: the ladder activates at +30 % ROI and locks +20 %, so the typical winner books **+20 % … +70 % ROI** (+1.6 % … +5.6 % equity), and only a small minority of runners reach the fixed TP.

## 4. Rule simulator — what the shipped TP/trail/SL actually do (`tools/rule_sim.py`)

`tools/rule_sim.py` drives the **real** exit code (`app/risk/manager.py:evaluate_trailing`,
`app/utils.py:stop_price_from_roi`) over martingale GBM paths (Itô term subtracted,
5–10 s ticks, 5 m ATR = 1.2 %, 10×, 3×ATR stop = 36 % ROI). Full write-up:
[`FINAL_RULES.md`](FINAL_RULES.md).

8 000 paths per cell (mean ROI on margin ± SE):

| Rule set | 0 bps drift | 30 bps drift |
|---|---|---|
| **shipped: TP 200 %, trail 30/20/10/10** | **+0.11 % ± 0.40** | **+1.13 % ± 0.40** |
| TP 200 %, trail 40/25/10/10 | +0.44 % ± 0.45 | +1.86 % ± 0.46 |
| TP 200 %, trail 45/30 + partial 50 % at +50 | +0.32 % ± 0.45 | +1.78 % ± 0.45 |
| TP 200 %, no trail | −0.03 % ± 0.55 | +2.12 % ± 0.56 |

Conclusions that must survive into any future edit of the rules:

* **At zero drift every geometry measures ≈ 0 %**, so the rules are drift-neutral: they
  shape the win/loss distribution, they do not create expectancy. The win rate of the
  signal is the only thing that decides the sign.
* Shipped geometry, measured: **mean win +29.6 % ROI, mean loss −35.7 % ROI → break-even
  win rate 54.6 %**; exit mix 44 % stop / 52 % trail / 4 % timeout. Because the average
  winner is smaller than the average loser, the bot must win *more than half* its trades
  to stand still. (Wider ladders: TP90/trail 40/25 → +38.5 %/−35.1 %, BE **47.6 %**;
  TP120/trail 50/25 + partial → +45.0 %/−34.4 %, BE **43.3 %**.)
* **The +200 % TP needs 16.7 ATR of travel** and fires on 0–4 % of simulated trades — it
  is a free outlier cap, not the money path. The trail is the money path.
* Numbers exclude venue fees except where stated (MEXC 0.4 %, Binance 1.0 %, KuCoin 1.2 %
  of margin per round trip), so the shipped geometry is **outright negative on Binance and
  KuCoin until the signal shows a real edge**.

> Trap: the first draft of the simulator used a zero *log*-drift walk, which drifts up by
> `exp(σ²/2)` per step and manufactured a +1.5–2.5 % ROI "edge" at every rule set. The fix
> (`drift = −½σ²`) is in `simulate_path`; **never quote a pre-fix number**.

## 5. Verdict

1. **The strategy can be net-positive, but the edge lives in the filters and the exit ladder — not the +200 % TP.** The parametric ladder prices the break-even win rate at **42.3 % (MEXC, ~45 % ROI stop)**; the rule simulator measuring the *shipped* geometry says **54.6 %** because the 30/20 trail banks winners around +30 % ROI. Both sit inside the plausible band for 5m divergence entries, so the filter stack and the chosen trail width are what decide whether the realised win rate clears the bar — see [`FINAL_RULES.md`](FINAL_RULES.md) for the measured table and the one-line config change that moves the bar to ~48 %.
2. **$20 → $10,000 in 7 days is not a realistic plan.** It needs +143 %/day (500x in 7 days), which requires either a >100 % win rate under trailing-realistic exits or ~42 consecutive clean TP hits with no losses; the honest probability is **under 1 %**, and the parametric reaches double digits only if you assume a 40–45 % win rate with the full +200 % TP filling on almost every winner. Treat that target as a stress metric, not a goal — the growth ladder in section 1 shows what the same edge does over 7/30/90/365 days instead.
3. **The real lever is how much of each winner you capture** — i.e. the trailing geometry. Wider trail steps move break-even down by 8–17 points (table above). The +200 % fixed TP is nearly irrelevant under the ladder because almost nothing survives that far, so do not tune it expecting a different outcome; tune `TRAIL_STEP_ROI` / `TRAIL_STOP_STEP_ROI` and the filter thresholds instead.
4. **Risk-of-ruin is dominated by concurrency.** 10 open positions at 8 % margin each is 80 % of equity deployed; a correlated alt-coin flush hits all of them at once. The drawdown kill-switch (40 %) and daily-loss halt (25 %) are what keep a bad day from becoming a terminal day — keep them enabled.

### Approximate per-trade probability estimates for AO 5m divergence (structure-break entry + 8 filters + 3 × ATR stop)

| Quantity | Estimate | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| P(price moves in favour ≥ 3 % before −4.5 %) | ~40–46 % | 5m oscillator-divergence studies cluster at 38–48 % before costs; the structure-break trigger and filters remove the worst cohorts, the trend/volatility filter removes chop |
| P(trade booked positive **after** the ladder) | ~45–55 % | any trade that prints +30 % ROI then retraces is closed at ≥ +20 % ROI, so the ladder converts some "would-be losers" into small winners |
| P(profitable after 50–60 trades) | ~50–60 % | section 3 — and only if the live win rate lands at or above the 42 % break-even line |
| P(500x in 7 days) | **< 1 %** (≈0 % under trailing-realistic exits) | sections 2–3; requires ~42 clean TP hits or a >100 % win rate |
